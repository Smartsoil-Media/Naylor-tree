### F. "Bray Cove Swimming Club / Naylor's Cove" — Pictorial History of Bray (IMG_3819)
- 19th-century cove was known locally as "Rocket Bank" (Volunteer Rocket lifesaving crew).
- "In the late 1880's **Bart Naylor**, a local fisherman and boat builder from the strand took a lease on the cove from the Towns Commissioners. He erected bathing boxes and a small pier to facilitate bathing and diving."

- ⚠ Important clarification: the book conflates two Bartholomews. The "Bart" who leased the cove in the 1880s was the **elder** Bartholomew (b. 1852); the Bart killed at the Somme in 1916 was his **son** (b. 1894). The web app already handles this correctly.

### J. Pictorial History of Bray (Cairns & Gallagher) — book extracts
- Title page (IMG_3839): *A Pictorial History of Bray Co. Wicklow, Volume One: The Seafront and Environs*, by Henry Cairns and Owen Gallagher.
- **Samuel Naylor Junior** entry (IMG_3840): A merchant seaman aboard the SS *City of Waterford*, sunk by collision with the Dutch tug *Thames* off Cardiff on the morning of Friday 19 September 1941 while bound for Lisbon. Crew of 23 transferred to the *Walmer Castle*, which was bombed by enemy aircraft at 4:30pm on Sunday 21 September 1941. *Walmer Castle* sank in mid-afternoon, taking 13 wounded with her. Among those killed: Captain Alpin, Bosun Kearney of Dunlaoghaire and **Samuel Naylor of Bray**.
- **Sam Naylor Snr** entry (IMG_3842): "Sam Naylor was one of four brothers — all fishermen; they all lived with their sister at **Rose Cottage**, Bray." Rowed a 25ft boat with 18ft oars to Ballygannon at 2am, four days a week. Fished codling, lobster, whelk, salmon (seine-netted, sometimes 120 in one shot), herring. Brother nicknames documented inside this text: **John = "Sketch"** (a lady artist sketched him); **Bert = "The Snipe"** (never wore a coat). Sam himself was nicknamed something like "Foley." Sam went deep-sea to Australia on the *Polly* with Dan Pluck, injured his hand and retired from the sea. Worked as a water bailiff on the Dargle for Lord Meath from Powerscourt to the sea. Built his last boat aged 80 on Dargle Road. Died aged 85.
- **Sam Naylor's 1923 fishing licence**: "FISHERIES No. 1 of DUBLIN DISTRICT. LICENCE TO FISH WITH A POLE AND NET FOR 1923. Issued to Samuel Naylor of Upper Dargle Road, Bray." Licence duty £2 0s.

## 3. The Catholic / non-direct Bray Naylors

The Bray Naylors split into Protestant and Catholic branches; the direct line is Protestant. From page 2 of Bart's memoir: Protestant nicknames included **Bart, Snipe, Coady**; Catholic side **Sam, Aboy, Joe**.

Confirmed identities (from Pictorial History of Bray + 1987 newspaper):

- **Sam Naylor Snr** ("The Fumbler" or similar) — fisherman, water bailiff. Lived at **Rose Cottage**, Bray, with three brothers and a sister. Died age 85.
  - Brothers: **John "Sketch" Naylor**, **Bert "The Snipe" Naylor**, and a fourth (unnamed in the text).
  - 1923 fishing licence at **Upper Dargle Road, Bray**.
- **Samuel Naylor Jr** — merchant seaman, killed 21 Sept 1941 when *Walmer Castle* was bombed (after the *City of Waterford* was sunk 19 Sept 1941).
- **Joe Naylor** (b. 7 May 1913, of 27 St. Kevin's Square, Bray) — nephew of Sammy. Coast-watching service WWII; chairman of Bray Rowing Club; United Bray Entertainers. Sister "Brody" alive in 1987. Interviewed in *Bray People* 31 July 1987.
- **Sammy Naylor** — Joe's uncle. "Upper deck, Sir" dental joke.
- **Tom Naylor** — referenced as "late Tom Naylor" in 1987 article (a Strand character).
- **Mick Naylor** — co-founder of Bray Cove Swimming Club, 1937.
- **Bill "Cody" Naylor** — photographed at the Strand c.1945.
- **Annie Naylor** — photographed c.1940 with Phil Devitt, Sam Naylor, Christy Fortune.
- **Rose Naylor** — "last of the Naylors" (Bray pictorial book). Married **Andy Donnelly**.

## 5. Bray context (for the Places page)

- 1859 — Martello Terrace built.
- 1860 — Mr O'Reilly of Bray offers £1,000 to construct the harbour.
- 1850s — William Dargan built the railway to Bray, developing it as a seaside resort.
- 1880 — Bray Sea baths opened.
- 1880s — Bart Naylor (the elder, b. 1852) leases the cove.
- 1901 — Dock Terrace census.
- 1916 — Bart Naylor Jr killed at the Somme; Edward Francis Hughes killed three days later.
- 1931 — Pool added to the Sea baths for water polo.
- 1935 — Bray Cove Swimming Club formed.
- 1937 — Bray Cove SC officially affiliated to IASA.
- 1941 — Samuel Naylor Jr killed (Walmer Castle).
- 1983 — Old Bray bridge collapsed.
